Accuracy notes & glossary
Exact restricted two-body Keplerian mechanics around a single point-mass body: Kepler's equation (ellipse), Barker's equation (parabola) and the hyperbolic Kepler equation are solved numerically each frame. There is no atmospheric drag, J2 oblateness, third-body gravity, or radiation pressure. Presets are illustrative — "Lunar transfer" is a simplified single-body ellipse, not a real patched-conic trajectory. Planet/Sun/Moon surface imagery is real equirectangular texture mapping (photographic/satellite-derived color maps), stylized here with a scrolling clip and a shading pass rather than a true spherical projection.
Periapsis / apoapsis
Closest and farthest points from the central body on an orbit.
Eccentricity (e)
Shape of the orbit: 0 = circle, 0–1 = ellipse, 1 = parabola, >1 = hyperbola.
Inclination (i)
Tilt of the orbital plane relative to the reference plane.
RAAN (Ω)
Right ascension of the ascending node — where the orbit crosses the reference plane heading north, measured around that plane.
Argument of periapsis (ω)
Angle from the ascending node to periapsis, measured in the orbital plane.
True anomaly (ν)
The vehicle's current angle from periapsis, measured in the orbital plane.
Specific orbital energy
Negative = bound (ellipse), zero = parabolic escape, positive = hyperbolic excess energy.
Hohmann transfer
The minimum-energy two-burn ellipse connecting two coplanar circular orbits.
Δv
The change in velocity a maneuver requires — the basic "cost" currency of spaceflight.
