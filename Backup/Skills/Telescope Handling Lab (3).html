Never point any telescope, finder or camera near the Sun without a certified solar filter fitted over the front of the instrument. Eye damage is instant and permanent.

Balance lab

An unbalanced mount strains the gears and drifts during tracking. Slide the counterweight until the beam sits level, then add a camera and balance again.

Checking

Counterweight 4.5 kg, tube 6.5 kg with the centre of mass 14 cm from the axis. Real balance also needs the Dec axis and the tube sliding in its rings.

Collimation lab

Looking down the focuser at a Newtonian, you want every circle sharing one centre. Your scope starts out knocked off by a hidden amount. Use the secondary and primary adjusters to centre it.

0%
Off centre
What you are changingThe secondary tilt aims the focuser's view at the middle of the primary. The primary knobs tilt the mirror so the reflection of the secondary sits at the centre of the view. When the focuser, secondary, primary and centre mark share one centre, the optical axis is aligned.

Cooldown estimator

Warm optics make shimmering air inside the tube and soft images. Estimate how long to leave the scope outside before you start observing.

0 min

This is a simple estimate, not a measurement. Real cooldown depends on telescope design, aperture, temperature difference, airflow, wind, insulation and observing conditions. The curve assumes the optics settle exponentially toward the outdoor temperature.

Quick reference

Before observing

  • Tripod secure
  • Mount secure
  • Counterweight secure
  • Telescope balanced
  • Finder aligned
  • Optics checked
  • Dew checked

During observing

  • Do not force the mount
  • Protect the optics
  • Watch the cables
  • Monitor dew
  • Never observe the Sun without proper solar protection

After observing

  • Cap the optics
  • Remove accessories
  • Secure the mount
  • Let cold gear warm up safely
  • Store dry
